## Overview

This research explores the development of an **intelligent reference validation framework** for identifying potentially hallucinated, invalid, or dangling references in scholarly publications.

The work focuses on using **Natural Language Processing and semantic analysis** to examine whether references cited within academic writing are contextually and semantically consistent with the claims or surrounding discussion in which they appear.

The research was initiated as part of my undergraduate thesis at **BRAC University**.

## Research Motivation

Accurate referencing is an important component of scholarly communication.

However, references may sometimes be:

- Incorrectly cited
- Contextually unrelated to the associated statement
- Incomplete or difficult to verify
- Semantically inconsistent with the surrounding text
- Hallucinated or nonexistent

These problems have become increasingly important with the growing use of automated writing and generative AI systems in academic and scientific workflows.

The broader goal of this research is to investigate how computational methods can assist researchers, reviewers, and editors in identifying potentially problematic references.

## Research Objective

The primary objective of the research is to investigate whether **contextual and semantic information** can be used to support automated scholarly reference validation.

Rather than relying only on bibliographic metadata, the research considers the relationship between:

- The surrounding scholarly text
- The context in which a citation appears
- The referenced academic work
- Semantic relationships between scholarly statements and references

The goal is to provide an intelligent mechanism for identifying references that may require further human verification.

## Research Approach

The research investigates a combination of **Natural Language Processing, semantic representation, and similarity-based analysis**.

At a high level, the work explores techniques involving:

- Scholarly text processing
- Semantic text representations
- Sentence-level contextual analysis
- Topic-level information
- Similarity-based reasoning
- Reference consistency analysis
- Evaluation of potentially invalid scholarly references

The implementation details and core research mechanisms are intentionally not published on this portfolio while the work remains part of ongoing research and publication activities.

## Dataset Development

A research dataset was constructed to support the investigation of scholarly reference validation.

The study considered scholarly reference information obtained from publicly accessible academic and research-related sources, together with additional experimental samples designed for evaluating reference-validation behavior.

The detailed dataset construction procedure is not publicly disclosed on this page.

## Evaluation

The proposed research framework was evaluated using a combination of computational analysis and validation procedures.

The evaluation was designed to investigate whether semantic and contextual information could help distinguish between references that appear consistent with scholarly discussion and references that may require further verification.

Detailed experimental configurations, thresholds, and implementation-specific evaluation procedures are reserved for the associated research manuscript.

## My Role

The project provided substantial experience in designing an NLP research workflow that moves beyond conventional text classification toward the analysis of relationships between scholarly statements and academic references.

## Research Significance

The work contributes to the broader problem of **research integrity and reliable AI-assisted scholarly communication**.

Automated reference validation could potentially support:

- Researchers checking citations before submission
- Academic reviewers evaluating manuscripts
- Editors screening scholarly documents
- AI-assisted academic writing systems
- Detection of potentially hallucinated scholarly references

The research is particularly relevant as generative AI systems become increasingly involved in academic writing and literature-related workflows.
